# Experimental Investigation of Hybrid Composites

Experimental investigation of the mechanical properties of flax and banana fiber hybrid composites using tensile, flexural, hardness, and impact testing.

## Project Overview

This project investigates the mechanical properties of natural-fiber hybrid composites developed using **flax fiber and banana fiber**.

The study evaluates how different proportions of flax and banana fibers influence the mechanical performance of the resulting hybrid composites. Five different fiber compositions were prepared and experimentally evaluated.

The project was conducted as an undergraduate Mechanical Engineering research study and resulted in a conference paper presented at **ICMech-REC 2023, NIT Warangal**.

## Objectives

The main objectives of the study were to:

- Investigate the mechanical properties of flax–banana hybrid composites.
- Compare different flax-to-banana fiber compositions.
- Evaluate tensile strength, flexural strength, hardness, and impact strength.
- Identify the composition offering the best overall mechanical performance.
- Explore the potential of natural-fiber hybrid composites as sustainable engineering materials.

## Materials

The study used:

- Flax fiber
- Banana fiber
- LY556 epoxy resin
- HY951 hardener

The resin-to-hardener ratio used was **9:1**, while the fiber-to-resin ratio was **1:3 by weight**.

## Hybrid Composite Compositions

Five compositions were investigated:

| Composition | Flax Fiber | Banana Fiber |
|---|---:|---:|
| FB1 | 20% | 80% |
| FB2 | 40% | 60% |
| FB3 | 50% | 50% |
| FB4 | 60% | 40% |
| FB5 | 80% | 20% |

## Methodology

The composite specimens were fabricated using the **hand layup method**.

The general fabrication process involved:

1. Drying the fibers to remove moisture.
2. Preparing the working surface using wax polish and a releasing agent.
3. Mixing LY556 epoxy resin with HY951 hardener.
4. Arranging the flax and banana fiber layers according to the selected composition.
5. Applying the resin-hardener mixture between the fiber layers.
6. Using a roller to remove trapped air and improve bonding.
7. Removing excess resin through the squeezing process.
8. Applying a laminate layer and maintaining a uniform load.
9. Allowing the laminate to remain undisturbed for 24 hours.

## Experimental Testing

The fabricated specimens were evaluated through four mechanical tests:

### 1. Tensile Test

Tensile testing was conducted to compare the resistance of the different hybrid composite compositions to tensile loading.

**Best reported result:**  
FB4 — **3.33 MPa**

### 2. Flexural Test

Flexural testing was used to evaluate the resistance of the composite specimens against bending loads.

**Best reported result:**  
FB1 — **322.92 MPa**

### 3. Hardness Test

Brinell hardness testing was performed to evaluate resistance to permanent deformation.

**Best reported result:**  
FB2 — **53.14 BHN**

### 4. Impact Test

Charpy impact testing was conducted to evaluate the ability of the hybrid composites to withstand shock and impact loading.

**Best reported result:**  
FB2 — **721.6 J/m²**

## Experimental Results

| Composition | Tensile Strength | Flexural Strength | Hardness | Impact Strength |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| FB1 | 3.04 MPa | 322.92 MPa | 34.36 BHN | 388.3 J/m² |
| FB2 | 3.13 MPa | 312.50 MPa | 53.14 BHN | 721.6 J/m² |
| FB3 | 2.95 MPa | 270.86 MPa | 28.15 BHN | 333.33 J/m² |
| FB4 | 3.33 MPa | 301.39 MPa | 17.53 BHN | 666.67 J/m² |
| FB5 | 2.67 MPa | 291.67 MPa | 25.13 BHN | 500 J/m² |

## Key Findings

- **FB4** achieved the highest tensile strength among the five compositions.
- **FB1** achieved the highest flexural strength.
- **FB2** achieved the highest hardness and impact strength.
- The results demonstrate that changing the flax-to-banana fiber ratio significantly affects the mechanical properties of the hybrid composite.
- Considering the combined performance across all four tests, the study identified **FB2 (40% flax / 60% banana)** as the best overall composition.

## Research Outcome

The study demonstrates the potential of combining flax and banana fibers to develop natural-fiber hybrid composites with different mechanical performance characteristics.

The findings can serve as a reference for further experimental work involving flax–banana hybrid composites and their potential engineering applications.
